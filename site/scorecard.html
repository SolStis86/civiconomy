Ownership Accountability Scorecard

Make ownership visible.

The Civiconomy scorecard is a practical framework for assessing how a business is owned, financed, governed, and operated.

Its purpose is simple: separate evidence from judgement and show whether enterprise is structured for long-term shared value.

Scoring scale

0

No meaningful evidence or serious concern.

1

Weak practice with limited positive evidence.

2

Basic practice, but major gaps remain.

3

Credible practice with room to improve.

4

Strong practice supported by clear evidence.

5

Leading practice and public example.

V1 categories

Ownership structure

Who owns the business, who has control rights, and whether ownership supports long-term health.

Worker voice

Whether workers have meaningful routes to information, participation, and representation.

Worker reward

Whether workers share fairly in enterprise success through pay, benefits, ownership, or profit sharing.

Debt and capital

Whether debt and capital are used to strengthen or weaken the operating business.

Reinvestment

Whether profits are reinvested into resilience, people, quality, systems, and long-term value.

Tax conduct

Whether the company contributes responsibly to the public systems it depends on.

Transparency

Whether stakeholders can understand ownership, finances, governance, and impact.

Community impact

Whether the business strengthens or weakens the places and systems it depends on.

Profile output

A scorecard profile should include a summary grade, category scores, evidence notes, positive indicators, warning signs, recommendations, and a confidence level.

Evidence notes

What is known, what is unknown, and where the data comes from.

Indicators

Positive signals and warning signs for each category.

Recommendations

Practical steps for improving ownership quality and accountability.

A company profile should show what is known, what is missing, and why a score has been assigned.
