# Worker Ownership Explainer

Worker ownership means workers hold a meaningful stake in the enterprise they help create.

It can take several forms. The right model depends on the company, sector, workforce, legal structure, and transition finance.

## Why Worker Ownership Matters

Worker ownership can help align the interests of workers, founders, customers, and long-term stewards.

It can support:

- better retention,
- stronger trust,
- shared upside,
- better information flow,
- long-term thinking,
- continuity during founder succession,
- stronger workplace culture.

## Common Models

### Employee Ownership Trust

A trust holds shares on behalf of employees. This can support broad-based ownership without every worker holding direct shares personally.

### Direct Share Ownership

Workers hold shares directly. This can create strong alignment, but needs careful communication, valuation, and liquidity planning.

### Cooperative Ownership

Workers collectively own and govern the enterprise using cooperative principles.

### Profit Sharing

Workers receive a share of profits without necessarily holding ownership rights.

### Hybrid Participation

A company may combine worker voice, profit sharing, employee trust ownership, founder transition, and external capital.

## Rights and Responsibilities

Worker ownership should come with clear rules:

- what workers own,
- how value is shared,
- how decisions are made,
- what information is available,
- how representatives are chosen,
- how disputes are handled,
- how ownership changes when workers join or leave.

## Key Principle

Worker ownership must be understandable. If workers cannot understand how the model works, it is not yet good enough.
