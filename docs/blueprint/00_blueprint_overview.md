# People-Powered Economy Blueprint

## Purpose

Civiconomy is a blueprint for moving from an extractive ownership economy toward a people-powered economy: an economy where capital serves civilisation, ownership distributes power, and enterprise is judged by what it builds rather than what it extracts.

The project exists to make private equity, wealth-concentration vehicles, and extractive commercial devices progressively obsolete by building better systems of capital, governance, ownership, accountability, and public legitimacy.

## Central Thesis

Private equity concentrates ownership to extract value. Democratic capital distributes ownership to compound value.

A people-powered economy does not rely on moral appeals alone. It must build institutions, funds, ownership pathways, public scorecards, legal structures, enterprise playbooks, and cultural narratives strong enough to compete with the existing financial order.

## Strategic Aim

The aim is not merely to criticise private equity. The aim is to replace its social function with better machinery:

- democratic capital funds,
- worker and community ownership pathways,
- founder succession alternatives,
- public-interest holding companies,
- stewardship trusts,
- cooperative infrastructure,
- transparent accountability systems,
- civic investment vehicles,
- policy reforms that reduce extraction and reward stewardship.

## Guiding Frame

This blueprint treats the economy as a democratic battlefield. Ownership determines who decides, who benefits, who is protected, and who is sacrificed. Therefore ownership must become a civic question, not merely a financial transaction.

## Core Output

The project should evolve into:

1. A public manifesto.
2. A practical institutional plan.
3. A democratic capital fund architecture.
4. A public education platform.
5. A scorecard for extractive ownership.
6. A founder and worker transition toolkit.
7. A policy reform agenda.
8. A movement brand capable of attracting citizens, workers, founders, unions, ethical investors, councils, philanthropists, and institutional partners.
