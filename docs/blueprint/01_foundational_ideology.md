# Foundational Ideology

## 1. Capital must serve civilisation

Capital is not sovereign. Capital is a tool. It should support human flourishing, ecological stability, productive enterprise, and democratic society.

## 2. Ownership is power

Ownership determines who decides, who benefits, who is protected, and who carries risk. It should be treated as a civic question, not only an investment question.

## 3. Extraction is not value creation

Financial engineering, wage suppression, excessive leverage, artificial tax avoidance, asset stripping, regulatory arbitrage, and political influence are not the same as genuine value creation.

## 4. Productive enterprise should be protected

Businesses that provide real goods, services, employment, infrastructure, care, knowledge, culture, food, energy, housing, or local capacity should not be treated as disposable financial instruments.

## 5. Workers are co-creators

Workers are not inputs. Any enterprise that depends on workers should share power, upside, information, dignity, and security with them.

## 6. Profit must be legitimate in source

Profit is valid when produced through genuine productivity, innovation, service, resilience, and stewardship. It becomes questionable when produced through dependency, monopoly, underpayment, environmental harm, deception, or public subsidy without public return.

## 7. Patient capital is superior to predatory urgency

Capital should match the life-cycle of the enterprise, not force the enterprise to match arbitrary exit timelines.

## 8. Democratic ownership must be practical

The project must build real mechanisms: employee ownership, steward ownership, cooperative ownership, profit sharing, worker voice, capped extraction, transparent governance, and accountable capital allocation.

## 9. Essential systems require public-interest duties

Healthcare, housing, education, water, energy, food, transport, care, and infrastructure should be governed with heightened responsibility.

## 10. Scale must not become domination

The movement may grow, but must not become another concentrated power bloc captured by wealthy patrons, political parties, consultants, asset managers, or insiders.
