# First Conversation Guide

This guide structures the first conversation with a founder who may be interested in a Civiconomy-style transition.

## Conversation Aim

Understand the founder's goals, the company's condition, and whether a better ownership pathway may be realistic.

## Opening Frame

Civiconomy is exploring routes for founders who want a fair transition while protecting the company, workers, customers, and long-term purpose.

## Questions to Ask

### Founder Goals

- Why are you considering succession or transition now?
- What do you most want to protect?
- What would a good outcome look like for you personally?
- What would a good outcome look like for the company?

### Business Context

- What does the company do?
- What makes it valuable?
- What are its main risks?
- How dependent is it on you as founder?
- Who are the key people needed for continuity?

### Workers and Culture

- How would you describe the workforce?
- Is there a trusted management team?
- Are workers already involved in decisions?
- Would employees be open to participation or ownership?

### Financial Shape

- Is the business profitable?
- What debt or obligations does it carry?
- What level of valuation are you expecting?
- Would you consider a phased exit?

### Ownership Direction

- Would employee ownership interest you?
- Would a purpose-protecting structure interest you?
- Would community involvement matter?
- Would you remain involved during a transition?

## Red Flags

- Founder only wants maximum price with no concern for continuity.
- Company is too dependent on one individual.
- Debt or liabilities make transition unrealistic.
- Workers are unlikely to support the model.
- Governance is unclear or disputed.

## Next Step

After the first conversation, classify the opportunity as:

- Strong candidate
- Possible candidate with work required
- Not currently suitable
- Needs more information
