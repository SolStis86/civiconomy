# Founder Transition Options

Civiconomy should help founders compare ownership transition routes without assuming one model fits every company.

## 1. Employee Ownership

A route where employees gain a meaningful stake in the company.

### Best suited for

- Stable operating businesses
- Strong worker knowledge
- Founders who want continuity
- Companies with a capable management layer

### Considerations

- Requires worker education
- Requires governance design
- Requires careful finance planning

## 2. Steward Ownership

A model where purpose and control rights are protected through a trust, foundation, or similar structure.

### Best suited for

- Mission-led companies
- Businesses with strong public or community purpose
- Founders who want purpose protection

### Considerations

- Requires specialist legal design
- May limit some future sale routes
- Needs clear stewardship rules

## 3. Community Ownership

A route where local people, users, or community institutions hold ownership or governance rights.

### Best suited for

- Local assets
- Essential services
- Place-based businesses
- Community infrastructure

### Considerations

- Requires local participation
- Requires governance capacity
- Funding may need to be blended

## 4. Civic Holding Company

A long-term holding structure that owns and supports businesses for stewardship rather than rapid resale.

### Best suited for

- Businesses needing patient capital
- Founder succession opportunities
- Companies with strong operating value
- Sectors where continuity matters

### Considerations

- Requires professional operations
- Requires capital discipline
- Requires transparent governance

## 5. Hybrid Transition

A mix of founder, worker, investor, steward, and community interests.

### Best suited for

- Complex companies
- Gradual transitions
- Cases where no single model fits

### Considerations

- More complex to govern
- Needs clear decision rights
- Needs plain-language stakeholder communication
